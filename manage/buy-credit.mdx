Credit is bought from the Billing screen in the dashboard. You choose the credit you
want, read the total before you commit, and pay on our payment provider's hosted
page.

## Before you start

You need two things:

- **The owner or admin role in the workspace.** Developers and viewers can see the balance and the statement, but they can't buy credit. If they try, the request is refused with HTTP 403 and this message: `Only the workspace owner or an admin can manage its billing: its saved cards and the buying of credit.` This check happens before checkout opens, so nobody gets partway through a purchase that can't finish.
- **At least 5.00 to buy.** That's the minimum credit you can receive in one purchase. Our fee, currently 0%, is added on top at checkout.

## How to

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Billing">
    Sign in to the dashboard and open **Billing**. The Balance tab is the default and
    is where the Add credit panel lives.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the credit you want">
    Pick one of the amounts offered, or type your own. The figure is the **credit you
    receive**, not the amount charged.

    An amount below the minimum is refused at the field: the refusal appears inline
    and the continue control stays disabled, so you never learn the minimum by being
    bounced back from a payment page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the three lines">
    The panel shows the same three lines whatever you type, and they move as you type:

    | Line | Example at 100.00 |
    |---|---|
    | Credit you receive | 100.00 |
    | Our fee, 0% | 0.00 |
    | You pay | 100.00 |

    The rate is read live from the dashboard rather than written into the screen, so
    what you see is the rate your checkout session will use. Where a card is already
    saved, it is named inside the same box, so the card, the total, the credit and
    the rate are read together before you commit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply a code, if you have one">
    **Have a code?** opens a single **Promo code** field with an **Apply** control,
    and one field serves both kinds of code. A Hopscotch credit code adds credit to
    your balance on its own, with no purchase, and the screen says so: the amount
    "added to your balance". A discount code from our payment provider is checked
    against the amount you typed and reduces the charge at checkout: the screen
    reads the code "applied" with the amount off. A credit code is redeemable once
    per workspace, and credit it grants is spent before credit you paid for.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Continue to payment">
    The control opens a checkout session with our payment provider and sends you to
    their hosted page. Nothing has been charged at this point: a checkout session is
    not a payment.

    Press it once. Each attempt opens its own checkout session, and two sessions can
    become two charges if both are paid.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pay">
    Enter the card on the hosted page. We never see the card number. If the card is
    declined, the hosted page is where you are told and where you can try another.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Come back">
    You are returned to the Balance tab, which says the purchase is **pending**. That
    is honest rather than cautious: credit is granted when the signed confirmation
    from the payment provider reaches us, which is usually seconds.

    You do not have to stay on the page. Closing it changes nothing either way.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## If something goes wrong

**You left the payment page**

No card was charged and no credit was added. The screen confirms this, and you can start again whenever you're ready.

Your saved card or Stripe Link wallet stays saved. No error anywhere in Hopscotch removes a saved payment method.

**The purchase couldn't start**

The amount you typed is still in the box. Don't press **Continue** again straight away. Every press opens a new checkout session, and the first one may have started after all. An open session costs you nothing unless you finish paying.

If you contact us, include the request id shown with the error.

**Your credit hasn't arrived**

Reload the **Balance** tab. Credit is added as soon as the payment is confirmed, and that happens automatically, so a paid purchase needs nothing more from you.

Still missing after a few minutes? Contact us with the payment id from your statement.

## Read the statement

The **Statement** tab lists every transaction, newest first, 50 per page. You can filter by date.

Each purchase appears as one line with three figures:

| Figure | What it shows |
| :-- | :-- |
| Amount charged | What your card paid |
| Credit granted | What was added to your balance |
| Fee | Our fee on that purchase |

Together, these explain any difference between your card statement and your Hopscotch balance.

**Running balance.** Each page shows a running balance: the total of your transactions up to that line. Use it to check our arithmetic.

For the amount you can actually spend, go to the **Balance** tab. That figure subtracts credit currently held for requests that are still running, so it can be lower than the running balance.

The Statement tab is live now. You'll find it next to **Balance** on the Billing screen, and it shows your real transaction record.

## Set the low credit notice

You can be emailed once when your available balance falls below a figure you choose.
The setting is on the Limits tab in Settings, and it is the owner's to change.

- The threshold is a whole number of minor units, from 0 to 100000. 500 means 5.00.
- **0 turns the email off.** The screen says so in words rather than leaving you to
  infer it from a number.
- You are emailed **once** per crossing. No second email is sent until the balance
  goes back above the figure and falls below it again.

The email states the balance you hold and the threshold you set, says that requests
are refused once credit runs out and that nothing is charged for a request we refuse,
and points at the Billing screen. It contains no link with a token in it, so nothing
about it is a credential and nothing about it expires.

## Your saved card

When you pay, our payment provider saves your card or Stripe Link wallet, so your next purchase doesn't ask for it again.

We don't store your card number or expiry date. Each time we show your card, we read the brand and last four digits straight from the provider, so the details you see are always current. A Link wallet shows as **Link**, with no last four digits.

## Auto reload

By default nothing charges your card while you are away, and the **Auto reload**
section on the Billing screen says so: "Off. You are only charged when you buy
credits yourself."

Turning it on takes two figures and a card. **When credits fall below** is the
threshold, and **Buy this many** is the credit bought each time the balance
crosses it. Both are dollar figures; the amount is at least the smallest purchase
we accept, and the threshold can be set to zero to turn the reload off again.
With no card on file the screen asks for one first, because a reload with nothing
to charge is not a reload. Once on, the summary line reads what will happen:
"On. Buys AMOUNT when the balance falls below THRESHOLD, so requests never stop."

A reload that is declined does not retry forever. After a run of consecutive
failed charges the reload stops, and the screen says so. When it stopped that way,
adding a new card resumes it; a manual purchase does not. The threshold is separate from the low credit
email's threshold below; the two do not have to agree.

Removing your last saved card switches Auto reload off. The removal sets its
threshold to zero and keeps the amount you chose to buy, and the removal's message
says so: "Automatic top-up has been turned off because no card is left to charge.
Add a card and switch it on again to resume." Unlike a stop after failed charges,
adding a card does not switch it back on here: you turn it on again yourself.

Setting either figure takes the same authority as buying credit: owner or admin.
