"Zero data retention" means two different things in this product, and this
page keeps them apart.

1. **A routing requirement.** Your workspace can require that a request served
   on our provider credentials goes only to a provider whose published
   retention is zero and whose training on inputs is disabled. This is shipped.
2. **A signed receipt about our own storage.** A statement, signed by us, of
   whether Hopscotch itself holds any of your prompt or response bodies. This
   is coming, and the last section says exactly how far it has got.

Neither changes what Hopscotch records about a request. We keep metadata and
never the bodies, with or without either of these, and
[Data and privacy](/manage/data-and-privacy) is the page for that.

## The workspace requirement

The requirement is a workspace setting. The dashboard describes it in one
paragraph, which is also the most precise description of what it does:

> When on, platform credentials use providers whose published retention is zero
> and training is disabled. Requests using your workspace's own provider key are
> always eligible. We cannot verify that provider's retention or training terms
> for your key.

Three consequences follow.

**It narrows which providers may serve a model.** A model is often carried by
more than one provider. With the requirement on, a request for that model is
served only by a provider recorded as zero-retention and no-training, and if no
such provider carries the model the request is refused rather than quietly
served elsewhere.

**Your own provider key is always eligible.** A request served on a key you
brought runs under your own agreement with that provider. We do not check that
agreement, and we do not refuse the request. See
[Bring your own key](/features/bring-your-own-key).

**"Recorded as" is the operative phrase.** The requirement reads what the
provider publishes about retention and training. It is a record we keep, not
a test we run against the provider, and this page makes no claim about what a
provider actually does with a request once it has it.

### What a refused request sees

A request the requirement blocks is refused before it is sent, with HTTP 403,
type `permission_error` and code `no_zdr_eligible_provider`. Nothing is
charged and no credit is held.

```json 403
{
  "error": {
    "message": "The workspace requires zero-data-retention providers, and \"openai/gpt-4o-mini\" is not served by one recorded as meeting that requirement. Turn the requirement off under Data handling, or route this request to a provider recorded as zero-data-retention.",
    "type": "permission_error",
    "param": null,
    "code": "no_zdr_eligible_provider",
    "request_id": "REQUEST_ID"
  }
}
```

The message names the model, so a developer can tell whether to pick another
model id or ask the workspace to change the setting.

### Turning it on

The setting is off until somebody sets it; the row reads "Not set yet" before
then. Turning it on or off takes owner or admin. Every member can read it.

The setting is published to the request path when it is saved, and the save is
refused rather than half-applied if that publication fails:

> The requirement could not be published to the request path just now, so it was
> not changed. Try again shortly.

### Checking a model before you rely on it

The models table marks each model's eligibility under the requirement:
eligible, eligible through your own key, not eligible, or eligibility
unavailable. The same answer is available to the dashboard for up to 40 model
ids at a time, so a screen can check a list before you commit to it. Those
marks sit behind the same switch as the setting row.

## Per key, through a guardrail

A [guardrail](/features/guardrails) carries its own zero-data-retention choice:
**On**, **Off**, or **Workspace default**. On a key the guardrail governs, that
choice replaces the workspace setting. So a workspace can leave the requirement
off and still hold one key to it, or require it everywhere and exempt one key
for a model nobody zero-retention carries. A guardrail refusal names the
guardrail instead of the workspace:

```text
The guardrail "eu-only" on this key requires zero-data-retention providers, and "openai/gpt-4o-mini" is not served by one recorded as meeting that requirement. Change the guardrail, or route this request to a provider recorded as zero-data-retention.
```

A guardrail can also require providers recorded as not training on inputs,
without the retention half. That refusal uses the same code with a message
that says "not training on inputs" instead.

## Signed receipts and your own storage bucket

<FeatureStatus
  missing="zero-data-retention receipts and a storage destination you own"
  instead="Both exist in the source tree behind the management API, which is not yet available to customers, and the receipt itself cannot yet say anything stronger than unverified. Nothing below is something you can do today."
/>

What is being built, so you know what to expect:

**A storage destination you own.** You will register a bucket of your own,
either an AWS S3 bucket reached through a role you create for us, or a
Cloudflare R2 bucket reached through a scoped token, plus a prefix within it.
We prove we can write to it before we keep the registration, with one
zero-byte object that we then delete, and we store the credentials encrypted.
Telemetry about your requests, never the bodies, is then written to your bucket
rather than ours. The AWS role must require an external id of `hopscotch:`
followed by your workspace id, so that nobody else's registration can assume
it.

**A receipt.** On request, we scan what we hold for your workspace and issue a
signed receipt: a verdict of `certified` or `unverified`, a closed list of
reasons, the counts behind them, a hash of the destination the receipt is
about, and an HMAC-SHA256 signature. Receipts are append-only and the most
recent hundred are listable. Disconnecting your bucket issues a termination
receipt and removes the credentials; it deletes nothing in your bucket.

**Why every receipt is `unverified` today.** A certified verdict needs a
plaintext scan of our own storage, and that scan is not built. Until it is,
every receipt carries the reason `plaintext_scan_unavailable`, and a receipt
that can only say unverified is not a promise we are willing to put on a
customer page. That is the gate on this section, and the stories that clear it
are named in the frontmatter of this page.

## What this page does not claim

- Nothing about what a provider does with a request once it has it. The
  requirement reads a provider's published terms and nothing else.
- No certification or audit of any kind. No such claim exists on this site.
- No setting that stores your prompts or replies. Capture of request bodies
  is not available in production, and the enable control refuses it there in
  as many words: the machinery it would store your prompts under is not built,
  so we will not take your consent for it.
