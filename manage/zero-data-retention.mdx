"Zero data retention" means two different things in this product, and this page keeps them apart.

1. **A routing requirement.** Your workspace can require that a request served on our provider
   credentials goes only to a provider whose published retention is zero and whose training on
   inputs is disabled. This is shipped.
2. **A signed receipt about our own storage.** A statement, signed by us, of whether Hopscotch
   itself holds any of your prompt or response bodies. This is coming; the last section says how far.

Neither changes what we record: metadata, never bodies. See
[Data and privacy](/manage/data-and-privacy).

## The workspace requirement

The dashboard describes the requirement in one paragraph, which is also the most precise
description of what it does:

> When on, platform credentials use providers whose published retention is zero and training is
> disabled. Requests using your workspace's own provider key are always eligible. We cannot verify
> that provider's retention or training terms for your key.

Three consequences follow.

**It narrows which providers may serve a model.** With the requirement on, a request is served only
by a provider recorded as zero-retention and no-training. If none carries the model, the request is
refused rather than quietly served elsewhere.

**Your own provider key is always eligible.** A request on a key you brought runs under your own
agreement with that provider, which we neither check nor refuse. See
[Bring your own key](/features/bring-your-own-key).

**"Recorded as" is the operative phrase.** The requirement reads what the provider publishes. It is
a record we keep, not a test we run, and this page makes no claim about what a provider actually
does with a request once it has it.

### What a refused request sees

A blocked request is refused before it is sent, with HTTP 403, type `permission_error` and code
`no_zdr_eligible_provider`. Nothing is charged and no credit is held.

```json 403
{
  "error": {
    "message": "The workspace requires zero-data-retention providers, and \"openai/gpt-4o-mini\" is not served by one recorded as meeting that requirement. Turn the requirement off under Data handling, or route this request to a provider recorded as zero-data-retention.",
    "type": "permission_error",
    "param": null,
    "code": "no_zdr_eligible_provider",
    "request_id": "REQUEST_ID"
  }
}
```

The message names the model, so a developer can pick another or ask for the setting to change.

### Turning it on

The setting is off until somebody sets it, and the row reads "Not set yet" before then. Owners and
admins change it; every member can read it.

### Checking a model before you rely on it

The models table marks each model's eligibility under the requirement: eligible, eligible through
your own key, not eligible, or eligibility unavailable. The dashboard can check up to 40 model ids
at a time.

## Per key, through a guardrail

A [guardrail](/features/guardrails) carries its own zero-data-retention choice: **On**, **Off**, or
**Workspace default**. On a key the guardrail governs, that choice replaces the workspace setting.
So a workspace can require the requirement everywhere and exempt one key, or leave it off and hold
one key to it. A guardrail refusal names the guardrail instead of the workspace:

```text
The guardrail "eu-only" on this key requires zero-data-retention providers, and "openai/gpt-4o-mini" is not served by one recorded as meeting that requirement. Change the guardrail, or route this request to a provider recorded as zero-data-retention.
```

A guardrail can also require providers not training on inputs, with the same code and a message
that says so.

## Signed receipts and your own storage bucket

<FeatureStatus
  missing="zero-data-retention receipts and a storage destination you own"

/>

**A storage destination you own.** You will register a bucket of your own: an AWS S3 bucket
reached through a role you create for us, or a Cloudflare R2 bucket reached through a scoped token,
plus a prefix. We prove we can write to it with one zero-byte object, which we then delete, and we
store the credentials encrypted. Telemetry, never bodies, is then written to it. The AWS role must require an external id of `hopscotch:` plus your workspace id.

**A receipt.** On request, we scan what we hold for your workspace and issue a signed receipt with a
verdict of `certified` or `unverified`, a closed list of reasons, the counts behind them, a hash of
the destination, and an HMAC-SHA256 signature. Receipts are append-only, and the most recent hundred
are listable. Disconnecting your bucket issues a termination receipt and removes the credentials. It
deletes nothing in your bucket.

**Why every receipt is `unverified` today.** A certified verdict needs a plaintext scan of our own
storage, and that scan is not built. Until it is, every receipt carries the reason
`plaintext_scan_unavailable`. A receipt that can only say unverified is not a promise we will put on
a customer page. The gate is named in this page's frontmatter.

## What this page does not claim

- Nothing about what a provider does with a request once it has it. The requirement reads a
  provider's published terms and nothing else.
- No certification or audit of any kind. No such claim exists on this site.
- No setting that stores your prompts or replies. Capture of request bodies is not available in
  production, and the enable control refuses it there.

See also [Security](/manage/security).
