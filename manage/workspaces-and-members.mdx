A workspace is the thing you sign in to and invite people into. It holds members, API keys,
usage records, a playground, and its own billing: one balance, one card, one statement.

Everything that spends is attached to a workspace. A key belongs to the workspace rather
than to the person who created it, and a request made with that key spends that
workspace's credit.

## Roles

Every member holds exactly one role, from a closed set: owner, admin, developer or viewer.
A role is a set of permissions rather than a rank, so read the table rather than assume it.

| What you can do | Owner | Admin | Developer | Viewer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| See the workspace and act inside it | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| See who is in it, and what each of them is | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| See the keys you are accountable for or named on | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| See every key in the workspace | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Create a key | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Revoke a key | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Limit a key to named members | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Invite somebody, and cancel a pending invitation | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Change what another member may do | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Remove a member | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Cancel a key reservation | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Add, replace or remove the workspace's own provider key | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Transfer the workspace to somebody else | Yes | No | No | No |
| Read the balance, the statement and whether requests are paused | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Buy credit and manage the saved cards | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Pause or resume requests, and set the low credit notice | Yes | No | No | No |

A developer may create a key, because a developer who cannot get a credential cannot use
the product. A viewer reads and creates nothing.

Two rules sit above the table and cannot be worked around by editing a role:

**Nobody can hand out authority they do not hold.** An attempt is refused with:
`You cannot give somebody permissions you do not have yourself.`

**Only the owner makes or unmakes an admin.** An admin creating a second admin would confer
persistence, and a stolen admin session must not be able to leave a permanent second
administrator behind. The refusal reads: `Only the workspace owner can make someone an admin.`

Your role is read at the moment you press a button, not when the screen loaded, so a demotion
in between is refused. Where your role does not carry an action, the answer is HTTP 403 with:
`Your role in this workspace does not allow that.` A workspace you are not a member of answers
as though it does not exist.

## Exactly one owner

A workspace has exactly one owner, always. Ownership is never granted. Trying to set somebody's
role to owner, or to move the current owner out of that role, is refused with:
`Ownership is transferred, never granted. Use the transfer control instead.`

Ownership moves by transfer, which promotes the new owner and demotes the outgoing one in the
same write, so there is never a moment with two owners or none. A removal or demotion that would
leave the workspace with no owner is refused, and the refusal names transfer as the way through.

## The owner and billing

Any member may read the balance, the statement, and whether the owner has paused requests.
Those three facts govern whether a member's own requests are served.

Buying credit and managing the saved cards belong to the owner and admins. Every billing control
answers a developer or a viewer with HTTP 403 and one sentence: `Only the workspace owner or an
admin can manage its billing: its saved cards and the buying of credit.`

Pausing or resuming requests and changing the low credit notice are the owner's alone, with
their own HTTP 403 sentence: `Only the workspace owner can pause or resume its requests, or
change when it is emailed about low credit.`

Billing belongs to a workspace, so one card cannot fund two workspaces. A workspace monthly spend
limit is not available yet; see [Spend controls](/manage/spend-controls).

## Keys and who can see them

A key belongs to the workspace and carries one accountable member, so spend can be attributed
to somebody. Being accountable for a key is not a permission: it says who answers for what the
key does, not who may use it.

A key may be limited to named members, which decides who can see and manage it. The owner and
admins always see every key. Everybody else sees the keys they are accountable for or named on,
and nobody outside the list learns that a hidden key exists. Every key list carries the same
fixed note, because one that appeared only when a key was hidden would announce it.

The limit is visibility and management, and nothing else. On the request path a key is a bearer
credential, so the limit is not per-person enforcement and promises no per-person usage split. A
developer or viewer attempting to set a limit is refused with: `Only an owner or an admin can
limit a key to particular members.` The one place a member limit does more than scope a screen is
the playground, where sending as a key counts as managing it. See [Playground](/features/playground).

Keys are created and ended on the Keys screen. See [Authentication](/get-started/authentication).

## Invitations

An owner or an admin invites somebody by email address and the role they should hold. An
invitation can offer admin, developer or viewer. It can never offer owner, because ownership
moves only by transfer. Only the owner can offer admin: an admin inviting a second admin would
confer persistence, not permission. An admin who tries is refused with
`Only the workspace owner can make someone an admin.`

- The link works for the invited address only, and is valid for seven days from when it was sent.
- Accepting requires signing in as that address, which proves the mailbox, so no separate
  verification is asked.
- The inviter's authority is re-checked at acceptance. An invitation sent by somebody who has
  since lost the right to send it does not work. It confers nothing until accepted.
- It may carry a key reservation, so the invitee arrives with a key waiting. The reservation takes
  one of the workspace's 500 key slots from the moment it is made. See
  [Authentication](/get-started/authentication#500-live-keys-per-workspace).
- A workspace may hold up to ten pending invitations at once.

A link that matches nothing and a link that belongs to somebody else's address answer
identically, so holding a session reveals nothing about who has been invited.

Cancelling a pending invitation takes the same permission as sending it, owner or admin, and
cancels any key reservation it carried, freeing the slot.

## Removing somebody never silently revokes a key

Removal is blocked until every live key the departing member is accountable for carries an
explicit choice. There is no default, because the safest-looking default is still a choice
nobody made.

- **Revoke** ends the key now. Anything still calling with it stops.
- **Keep** leaves the key working and names a different member as accountable. The new
  accountable member must be somebody who could revoke it.

An unrecognised choice refuses the whole request. Before you decide, the product shows each live
key and its recent activity. The removal happens completely or not at all. A kept key can carry a rotate-by date, which is a reminder to replace it and never a kill switch.

Exactly three things revoke a key, and the list is closed: somebody revoked it; it was replaced,
which mints a new key and ends the old one in one motion; or the member was removed and the
remover chose revoke. A role change never revokes a key, and neither does a lapsed reservation,
because a reservation is not yet a key. Hopscotch staff can also revoke a customer key. That is
recorded against a named member of staff, not as a fourth way for a workspace to end its own key.

There is no documented self-removal flow for leaving a workspace. An owner or admin removes people.

## How to

### Create a workspace

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the workspace control">
    The control near the top of every screen opens each workspace you belong to, oldest joined
    first, with **New workspace** at the foot. That is the only door to a workspace that does
    not exist yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose New workspace">
    The list becomes a small form, with the name field focused.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    The form has one field, **Name**. You can rename a workspace later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create it">
    Press **Create workspace**. You become its owner in the same step and land on its Usage screen.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A name must trim to 1 to 40 characters (`A workspace name is 1 to 40 characters.`) and must differ
from every workspace you already belong to, ignoring case. Two customers can each open a workspace
with the same name, but a duplicate of one of yours is refused with `You are already in a workspace
with that name.` What you typed stays in the box after a refusal.

The form warns you first: `A new workspace has its own balance starting at zero, its own keys and
its own members, and you will be its owner.` It shares no credit with your other workspaces. There
is no limit on how many you can create, own or belong to.

### Invite somebody

**Who can do this:** owner or admin. Use the Team screen and the rules under
[Invitations](#invitations).

### Change what a member may do

**Who can do this:** owner or admin.

- **Never to or from owner.** Use the transfer control.
- **Only the owner makes or unmakes an admin.**
- **Never above your own authority.** A role change never revokes a key. Moving somebody to viewer
  leaves their keys working, and they keep answering for them until somebody changes that.

### Transfer the workspace

**Who can do this:** the owner only. It is the one thing an admin cannot do.

The transfer promotes the new owner and demotes you in one write. It also hands over the card,
the buying of credit and the ability to pause requests. Read
[the owner and billing](#the-owner-and-billing) before you press it.

### Remove somebody

**Who can do this:** owner or admin. Follow
[Removing somebody never silently revokes a key](#removing-somebody-never-silently-revokes-a-key).
A removal that would leave the workspace with no owner is refused. Transfer first, then remove.

## What we did not verify

The create, invite, role-change, transfer and removal flows are described from the rules the
service enforces. They have not been walked end to end against a deployed environment for this
page. If a screen disagrees with this page, trust the screen and tell us.
