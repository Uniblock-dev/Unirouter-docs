Hopscotch runs on prepaid credit, with no month-end invoice. Two rules cover most of it:

1. **Our fee is added when you buy credit.** It is never applied to a request.
2. **A served request deducts what the model costs at the provider's rate, at face value.**
   We add nothing to it.

## Buying credit

You choose the **credit you receive**. Our fee is added on top and the total is charged. At
the current rate of 0%:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Credit you receive | 100.00 |
| Our fee, 0% | 0.00 |
| You pay | 100.00 |

The smallest purchase is 5.00 of credit. The rate in force when checkout starts is recorded
on that purchase, so a later change cannot reprice it. Credit is never rounded; the fee is
rounded half up to the cent, once. The Billing screen is the authority for your next purchase.

Credit is granted only on our payment provider's signed confirmation, never on a browser
redirect. Until it arrives, the purchase shows as pending.

## What a request costs

A served request is charged what its model costs at the provider's rates, at face value, with
no markup and no fee at request time. Charges are computed per usage type (input, cached input,
cache writes, output and reasoning tokens), and the rates behind each charge are recorded with it.

Charges are stored in integer **micro-dollars** in USD, so a small request is not rounded away.
Figures **you** type, such as the spend rate cap, the low balance threshold and a key's credit
limit, stay in minor units, where 100 is 1.00.

## The three figures on your balance

| Figure | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Available** | What you can spend now. A new request is admitted against this figure. |
| **Settled** | The sum of every transaction on your statement. |
| **Held for requests in flight** | Credit set aside for requests that are still running. |

Available is settled minus held. A request is admitted against a hold for the most it could cost.
When it finishes, the hold comes back and you are charged for what it used. Omitting `max_tokens`
holds more, which is why such a request can meet the spend rate cap sooner. See
[Rate limits and spend controls](/manage/spend-controls).

One component computes every balance, so your balance and usage figures cannot disagree.

## Running out of credit

**Nothing is charged for a request we refuse.** When available balance cannot cover a new
request, it is refused with HTTP 402 and code `insufficient_credit`. The message names both
figures:

```json
{
  "error": {
    "message": "This account has $0.54 available and the request needs $0.55 held.",
    "type": "insufficient_quota",
    "param": null,
    "code": "insufficient_credit",
    "request_id": "req_XXXXXXXXXXXX"
  }
}
```

A request already streaming when credit runs out is **cut off**. The stream ends with a `data:`
line carrying the same envelope, with type `insufficient_quota` and code `balance_exhausted`. The hold goes back. If no token
counts arrived, you are charged nothing. The codes differ on purpose: a cut stream and a request
that never started are different events. Buying credit clears both.

## Every transaction is on your statement

The Statement tab lists every transaction, newest first, fifty lines to a page. Each line shows when, the
signed amount in minor units, its kind, and a quotable id: the payment id on a purchase, the
request id on a charge. A **purchase line** carries the amount charged, the credit granted and the
fee between them, which explains any gap between your card statement and your balance. A
**running balance** on each page is the sum up to that line. It is not your available balance.

Some charges carry one of three sentences, quoted exactly:

- `Estimated charge, token counts were not confirmed`
- `The provider did not report token counts, so the estimate was charged`
- `Charged after the hold for this request had expired`

## Who can do what

<OwnerOnlyNote />

Any member may read the balance and the statement. Buying credit and managing the card belong to
the owner and admins, and the low credit setting to the owner alone. Card controls live on the
Billing screen's Payment tab. A developer or viewer calling a billing route gets HTTP 403.

## What this product does not do

**It never charges your card unless you asked.** Auto reload is off by default. See
[Buy credit](/manage/buy-credit#auto-reload).

**We never see your card number.** Our payment provider holds it. Removing a saved card or Stripe Link
wallet changes nothing about credit you already bought.
