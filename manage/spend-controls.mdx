Several limits can refuse a request, each with its own code, so you can tell "too
fast for this key" from "too much spend attempted too quickly" from "this key is done".

**Nothing is charged for a request we refuse**, whichever limit refused it.

## The controls

Five controls decide how fast this workspace can spend, and when it stops. Four
live in **Settings**, under **Limits**; the per-member limit is on the **Team** screen's
**Spend** tab.

| Control | What it does | Who may read it | Who may change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pause | Refuses every request straight away, revoking nothing. | Any member | Owner |
| Spend rate cap | Limits how much credit may be held per rolling 60 seconds. | Any member | Owner |
| Monthly workspace limit | Caps what the whole workspace may spend in a UTC calendar month. Not available yet. | Any member | Owner |
| Monthly member limit | Caps what one member's keys may spend in a UTC calendar month. Not available yet. | Any member | Owner |
| Low credit notice | Emails you once when your available balance falls below a figure. | Any member | Owner |

A [guardrail](/features/guardrails) belongs to one key rather than to the workspace,
and two limits below also belong to a single key. Only that key is refused. The
rest of the workspace keeps serving.

## The refusals

| Status | `code` | What ran out | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 429 | `rate_limit_exceeded` | This key's request rate. | Wait the seconds in `Retry-After`. |
| 429 | `spend_rate_exceeded` | Your spend rate cap, which counts credit held rather than spent. | Wait `Retry-After` seconds, up to 60, or send `max_tokens`. |
| 429 | `upstream_rate_limited` | Nothing of yours. The model's provider rate-limited the call. | Wait the seconds in `Retry-After`. Nothing is wrong with the request and nothing is charged. |
| 413 | `payload_too_large` | Nothing. The body is over the 8388608 byte maximum this API accepts. | Send fewer messages, a shorter prompt, or fewer inputs. |
| 402 | `insufficient_credit` | Available credit at admission. | Buy credit. |
| 402 | `key_limit_exceeded` | This key's own credit limit, for its current window. | Wait for the window to roll, raise the limit, or send on another key. |
| 402 | `member_limit_exceeded` | Not available yet. Your own monthly spend limit. | Ask the owner to raise or clear it on the Team screen's Spend tab. |
| 402 | `workspace_limit_exceeded` | Not available yet. The workspace's monthly spend limit. | Ask the owner, who sets it on the Settings screen under Limits. |
| 402 | `key_limit_exceeded` with a guardrail named | A spend cap set by a [guardrail](/features/guardrails) on this key. | Ask an owner or admin to change or detach the guardrail. |
| 401 | `key_expired` | Nothing. The key's own expiry date has passed. | Create a new key. Expired keys are never renewed. |
| 503 | `paused_by_owner` | Nothing. The workspace owner has paused requests. | Lift the pause in Settings. |

Every refusal uses the standard error envelope. Branch on `code`, since messages may
be reworded. One further 429 meaning, for a provider pool we are protecting, is specified
but not yet shipped. Until it ships, retry a 429 with none of these codes after a short
pause, and quote the request id if it persists.

## Pause every request

Pause is the control for a runaway client or a weekend you would rather not be
surprised by. It revokes nothing, so lifting it starts everything again.

Before you press it, the screen states the effect in numbers:

> Pausing refuses every request on all N live keys straight away, with nothing charged
> for a refused request. Over the last 7 days this account made N requests, which is
> the traffic that would have stopped. Nothing is deleted and no key is revoked.

The request count is the trailing seven days of actual traffic, not a forecast.

While paused, requests are refused with HTTP 503 and code `paused_by_owner`, and
nothing is charged for them. Credit already held for requests that were running is
still theirs. **Requests already in flight are not cut off.** Each finishes and is
charged for what it really used.

Any member may read whether requests are paused. Only the owner may press or lift it,
and lifting takes effect on the next request.

<Note>
  Pause is yours to lift. It is a different state from an account we have suspended,
  which has its own answer and cannot be lifted from this screen.
</Note>

## Spend rate cap

The spend rate cap limits how much credit may be **held** per rolling 60 seconds.
A new account starts at 100000 minor units (1000.00). An account opened before
that default was raised keeps the cap it already holds, which is 5000 minor units
(50.00) unless somebody changed it. Over the cap, requests are refused with HTTP 429 and code
`spend_rate_exceeded`, carrying `Retry-After` in whole seconds, and they are admitted
again within 60 seconds with nothing for you to do.

Setting the cap is the owner's. Two rules apply:

- **A cap of 0 is refused.** Stopping requests entirely is what the pause is for.
- **Lowering always works**, to any figure at or above 1 minor unit. Raising stops at
  the self-service ceiling until we raise it.

### What it counts, which is not what you spent

When a request is admitted we hold the most it could cost. That figure is the
request's own `max_tokens` where it names one, and the model's published maximum
output length where it does not. The request then settles for what it produced, and
the rest of the hold comes back.

The window counts what was held, and nothing is taken back out of it. A released,
expired or settled hold all count for their full 60 seconds. That is deliberate: a
leaked key making thousands of small requests holds almost nothing at any instant.

Two consequences follow:

- A minute of ordinary traffic that all completed still counts against the ceiling
  for a minute afterwards.
- On an expensive model, requests that omit `max_tokens` hold the model's whole
  published maximum output. The cap can bind well below its own figure in real
  spending a minute, because the credit it counts is credit you were never going to spend.

The refusal's message says "reserved", which means held. The cap clears 60 seconds
after the earliest hold counted, and `Retry-After` is computed from that same hold.

### The lever: send `max_tokens`

A client that names a realistic ceiling gets several times more requests a minute out
of the same cap, **and is charged exactly what it was before**, because the charge has
always been for what was produced.

```python Python
completion = client.chat.completions.create(
    model="openai/gpt-4o-mini",
    max_tokens=400,
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": "Say hello."}],
)
```

If you are running a batch job, a load test or an evaluation sweep, change this first.

### Out of credit and over the cap at the same time

You are told about the balance first. An account both out of credit and over its
ceiling gets the 402, because the cap clears in 60 seconds and telling someone with no
credit to slow down is advice that cannot work.

## How to

### Set your spend rate cap

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Limits section in Settings">
    Each row names the unit it is enforced in and says whether it is yours to move.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the ceiling">
    The spend rate cap field states the highest value you may set yourself. Where we
    cannot state it at that moment, the hint says so and adds that lowering still works.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter an amount">
    At least 1 minor unit, which is one cent. A figure above the stated ceiling is refused,
    naming both your figure and the highest you may set, and nothing is written.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    The new cap applies to the next request on this workspace. There is nothing to deploy
    and nothing to expire.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Monthly limits, for the workspace or for one member

Monthly limits are not available yet, and no request is refused by either one. When
they are, both count spend from your balance in the current UTC calendar month and refuse
at admission, before credit is held. The workspace limit is set by the owner alone, and
zero clears it. A member limit stops only that member's keys. Neither counts a request
served on your own provider key. See [Bring your own key](/features/bring-your-own-key).

## Limits that belong to one key

**A key may carry its own expiry date.** A key presented after it is refused with HTTP
401 and code `key_expired`, and the message names the instant it expired. It is checked
before the per-key request rate. Nothing renews a key: create a new one on the Keys
screen. The code is not `invalid_api_key` because the advice is opposite: an invalid key
needs the right key, an expired one needs replacing.

**A key may also carry its own credit limit.** A request that would take the key past
it is refused with HTTP 402 and code `key_limit_exceeded`, before any credit is held. The
message names what that key has settled and holds in its current window. Unlike
`insufficient_credit`, this stops only that key, and buying credit does not clear it. The
window rolling over clears it, or somebody raising or removing the limit on the Keys
screen. A limit that never resets does not clear by waiting.

Requests served on your own provider key do not count against a key's credit limit and
are never refused by it. Nothing is charged when either limit refuses, and no credit is
held.

## Low credit notice

The low credit notice emails you once when your available balance falls below a figure
you choose. A value of 0 turns the email off, and the screen says so in words. The setting
and the email are described on [Buy credit](/manage/buy-credit#set-the-low-credit-notice).

## Which limits you can raise

The Limits section marks each limit as yours or shared:

> This protects your own money. You may raise it yourself, up to the limit shown.

applies to the spend rate cap, and

> This protects a provider account we share across every customer. Raising it is a
> decision we make, not one you can make yourself.

applies to the request and token rates. Raising a shared rate trades away another
customer's room, so there is no self-service path. If you need more room, ask us.

Some rows read "Not yet enforced. We are not checking your traffic against this figure
yet." A row with no figure means nobody is checking it, never that you have no room.

## Per-key request rate

Each API key has a request rate, counted per minute. Over it, the request is refused
with HTTP 429 and code `rate_limit_exceeded`, with a `Retry-After` header carrying the
seconds to wait. The count is per key, so splitting work across two keys splits the
counter. The figure in force is shown in the Limits section of Settings.
